Sails Protocol is built from nine composable primitives. Every financial transaction — a P2P trade, an escrow, a dispute — is expressed using combinations of these primitives. Rather than hard-coding a single workflow, the protocol defines atomic contracts that any module can implement, allowing new use cases (lending, swaps, agent-driven automation) to be built on the same foundation without rewriting the core.

## The Nine Primitives

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Identity" icon="key" href="/concepts/identity">
    **WHO is participating.** Every participant is an Ed25519 keypair — self-sovereign, portable across wallets and platforms, with no centralized registry. Your keypair is your identity.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Intent" icon="bullseye" href="/concepts/intents">
    **WHAT someone wants to happen.** An Intent is a machine-readable expression of a desired outcome — "buy 0.01 BTC at most $46,000 via PIX" — rather than an imperative command. The protocol coordinates around it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Discovery" icon="magnifying-glass" href="/concepts/protocol-primitives">
    **WHO ELSE this could happen with.** Discovery matches Intents with available offers and counterparties via P2P (HyperDHT) or an order book, without any central authority owning the matching process.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Negotiation" icon="comments" href="/concepts/protocol-primitives">
    **HOW terms get agreed.** Two identities reach agreement over a trade channel. Today that's a real-time chat channel relayed by the node (`openp2p.chat()`); structured offer/counter-offer negotiation and agent-to-agent negotiation are part of the protocol design but not implemented yet.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Settlement" icon="lock" href="/concepts/settlement">
    **HOW value actually moves.** Pluggable settlement providers lock and release funds according to agreed terms. Custody depends on the provider: client-held-key providers need two of three keys to move funds, while `WDK_USDT_EVM` is a disclosed server-custodial reference implementation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reputation" icon="star" href="/concepts/reputation">
    **WHY to trust a counterparty.** An outcome-based trust score computed from real settlement outcomes and tied to an Identity — not to any platform, app, or email address. Ratings never move it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Agent" icon="robot" href="/concepts/protocol-primitives">
    **WHO (or what) assists a participant.** Local AI (QVAC) drafts intents and offers, assesses risk, flags social-engineering patterns, and can propose advisory dispute rulings. It never negotiates, signs, or moves funds on anyone's behalf.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Proof" icon="shield-check" href="/concepts/protocol-primitives">
    **HOW a claim gets verified.** A structured `Claim → Proof → Verification` chain represents verifiable assertions for off-chain events — payment receipts, collateral proofs, delivery confirmations — in a single consistent format any module can consume.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dispute" icon="gavel" href="/concepts/protocol-primitives">
    **HOW a disagreement gets formally resolved.** An assigned arbiter reviews both parties' evidence (optionally alongside an advisory AI proposal) and issues a ruling signed with their own key. A resolved dispute always produces a Settlement outcome: release, refund, or split.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How They Compose: A Full Trade

The following walkthrough shows how the nine primitives chain together in a typical P2P BTC/fiat trade.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Seller establishes Identity and publishes an offer">
    The seller creates an Ed25519 Identity and publishes a sell offer via the **Discovery** primitive (OpenLiquidity). The offer lands in the node's offer book, and can optionally be broadcast to connected peers over HyperDHT.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buyer expresses a TradeIntent">
    The buyer creates a `TradeIntent` — "BUY 0.01 BTC, max $46,000, via PIX" — through the **Intent** primitive. The Intent Engine routes it to the Discovery layer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Parties agree on the details">
    Discovery finds offers within the Intent's limits and surfaces a match for the buyer to approve. The two parties open the trade's **Negotiation** channel and agree on the payment details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Settlement escrow is created and locked">
    With terms agreed, the **Settlement** primitive creates an escrow (e.g. a 2-of-3 Bitcoin multisig). The seller funds and locks it; buyer and seller keys stay on their own devices.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Buyer submits payment Proof">
    The buyer sends fiat directly to the seller (outside the protocol) and records a **Proof** — a `payment_sent` Claim with the payment receipt as evidence — then marks the payment as sent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Escrow is released — Reputation updated">
    The seller confirms receipt and signs the escrow release. The BTC settles to the buyer's wallet. Both parties' **Reputation** scores are updated automatically from the trade outcome.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If contested: Dispute process begins">
    If either party disputes the outcome, the **Dispute** primitive is triggered: an arbiter is assigned, both parties submit evidence, and the arbiter's signed ruling resolves back into a Settlement state transition.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Two additional components — the **Capability Registry** and the **Policy/Rules Engine** — work across all nine primitives without being primitives themselves. The Capability Registry records what each participant has consented to (for example creating intents or moving escrow funds), and production nodes enforce it. The Policy Engine applies rules that govern how primitives behave. Neither has its own participant-facing lifecycle, which is why they are Core components rather than a tenth and eleventh primitive.
</Note>
